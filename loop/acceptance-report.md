## `kernel/LAWS.bend` against the ticket texts

Every law a ticket names is stated and proven, with these differences:

- **Ticket 02, `bytes.tbcd.length` and `bytes.short_input_fails`.** Ticket 34 deleted `tbcd_digits`, which the kernel never called: the length law is stated over `B.Tbcd.decode`, which reads a whole IE body, and the short-input conjunct about `tbcd_digits` went with it.
- **Ticket 07, `kdf.snn.nonempty`.** Stated over `Ausf.snn(Cfg.plmn(cfg))` for a valid configuration (ticket 34); `kernel.config.valid` shows every state holds no other.
- **Ticket 07, `kdf.length`.** The ticket says every derived key is 32 bytes; the law gives K_NASint and K_NASenc 16 bytes, which is what TS 33.501 Annex A.8 derives (the low 128 bits). The law is right; the ticket's wording is loose.
- **Tickets 10, 11, 13, 14, `*.suffix`.** The ticket shape says "`rest` is a suffix of `bs` and the consumed prefix is exactly the declared length". For readers of whole messages the law says the input bytes are exactly the encoding of what was read, which implies both. `pfcp.ie.suffix` and `nas.payload_container.suffix` keep the ticket's shape.
- **Ticket 11, `nas.epco.encode_bounded`.** Stated for valid ePCO (each container at most 255 bytes) rather than by a length-indexed container type. The Accept's ePCO is valid by `smf.epco.accept_valid` (ticket 34).
- **Ticket 13, `ngap.no_big_nat`.** Was never stated before this ticket; it is now (line 963) and proven in `kernel/proofs/ngap_view.bend`: AMF-UE-NGAP-ID decodes to at most five canonical octets, BitRate to octets in either form. Both are byte lists by type.
- **Tickets 17 and 18, `amf.auth.guarded` and `amf.registration.completes_or_releases`.** Deleted in ticket 33: both held by the shape of `Phase` and said nothing of a timer running. `amf.guard.expires` states what a guard does when it fires.
- **Ticket 18, `amf.release.forgets_ue`.** Ticket 33 states "no later action can mention it" as the law, over every event. What it said before, that the owner's Release Complete leaves no context or session under the key, is kept as `amf.release.complete_forgets`.
- **Ticket 18, `amf.indexes.unique`.** UE IPv4 and SEID are session indexes and are covered by `smf.ip_pool.unique`; "every entry points to an existing context" holds because the indexes are the context table itself.
- **Ticket 14, `pfcp.fteid.decode_valid`.** "Its length matches its flags" is stated as: what decodes re-encodes to its body, for a body of bytes (ticket 34); `pfcp.fseid.decode_valid` says the same of the F-SEID.
- **Ticket 19, `smf.establish.reject_leaves_state`.** "Changes no SMF state" is stated as sessions and PFCP sequence unchanged; the UE's NAS uplink COUNT advances. The pool is part of validation (ticket 34), and so are the TEID and SEID drawn (ticket 35).
- **Ticket 19, `smf.establish.failure_releases`.** Covered two of the four later failures before ticket 23, all four since; since ticket 34 the gNB branch holds for any failed list naming the session; since ticket 38 every branch past Creating states the PFCP Session Deletion over `smf.sessions.known`, and the guard branch holds for any session the timer finds.
- **Ticket 19, `smf.packet_filters.capacity_reject`.** An invariant over the Accept every creating session holds (ticket 34); it holds through `smf.modification.rejected`: nothing can add filters.
- **Ticket 19, `smf.teid.unique`.** Stated over any table where `Sessions.unique` holds (ticket 34), which `smf.ip_pool.unique` gives for every reachable state.
- **Tickets 35 and 38, `state.ids.free`.** Stated in ticket 35, with option A of ticket 38's blocker: guard timers are their row's key, AMF-UE-NGAP-IDs odd and local SEIDs even and not zero, and an InitialUEMessage is refused when no AMF-UE-NGAP-ID is free. Stronger than the ticket: a key that holds a context after a step and none before is the one the step gives. `smf.sessions.known` is stronger than ticket 38: eUPF knows a session exactly when it is past Creating.
- **Ticket 35, `smf.request.retransmitted`.** Item 1 for the session requests: a Session Establishment or Modification Request goes again unchanged, sequence number included, up to N1 = 3 times; `smf.establish.failure_releases` states the guard's failure paths for those two phases at the retry limit. Session Deletion Requests follow in ticket 39: `smf.deletion.retransmitted` and `smf.deletion.keyed`.
- **Ticket 35, `amf.ue_key.unknown`.** Item 4 as a law: an AMF-UE-NGAP-ID of 2³² or more in any of the five UE-associated messages changes no state, with Error Indication and a log line as the only actions; `amf.ue_context_release.owner_only` and `amf.nas.plaintext_after_security` name the UE through `Ngap.of_big(amf)`, so they hold of every ID.
- **Ticket 35, `smf.guard.stopped`.** Not asked for by a ticket: a step that removes a session row stops its guard timer, so a stale timer never ends a later session under the same SEID. `amf.guard.expires` now lists those stops after the deletions.
- **Ticket 33, `amf.release.forgets_ue`.** The key exempted is the one the step gives a new context, `St.State.fresh(st)`, which replaces `next_ue(st)`.
- **Ticket 39, `amf.ng_setup.first`.** Item 8: from an association that has not completed NG Setup, every message but NG Setup, InitialUEMessage and every UE-associated message included, changes no state, with Error Indication and a log line as the only actions. The laws that state the exact answer to a UE-associated message (`amf.initial_ue.reject_leaves_state`, `amf.ue_key.unknown`, `amf.release.complete_forgets`, `amf.handover.responses_correlated`, `amf.handover.released_side_completes`, `smf.establish.reject_leaves_state`, `smf.modification.rejected`, the gNB clause of `smf.establish.failure_releases`) assume the association is set up; for the other case `amf.ng_setup.first` gives the same unchanged state with a different cause, so each advisory row they rule out stays ruled out.
- **Ticket 39, `smf.deletion.retransmitted` and `smf.deletion.keyed`.** Item 2, the last part of ticket 35 item 1: a Session Deletion Request is pending under its own guard from the step that sends it until answered, goes again unchanged, sequence number included, up to N1 = 3 times, and is then given up; its guard timer, the session's SEID with the top bit set, is no context's, session's or the N4 association's in any reachable state. The law states the retransmission for a pending deletion; that every Session Deletion Request is recorded pending holds by construction (`Up.N4.deletions` builds the request and the pending row from the same bytes) and is checked by the tests, not stated as a law.
- **Naming.** `aper.padding.zero` was renamed `aper.pack.padding_zero` in this ticket so it no longer sits one character from `aper.padding_zero`.
- **Extra laws** beyond the tickets: `amf.handover.released_side_completes` (1990: after an N2 handover the released side's UE Context Release Complete changes no state and gets no Error Indication), `amf.ue_key.unknown` (ticket 35 item 4, outside the advisory log: `amf.ue_context_release.owner_only` alone rules GHSA-mg8r out), `bytes.nibbles.roundtrip`, `plmn.*`, `nas.mobile_identity.guti_length`, `nas.payload_container.short`, `frame.event.decode_valid`, `aper.pack.padding`, `aper.pack.padding_zero`, and the generated `.suffix` law per NGAP type.

Five laws assume an invariant of the state before the step, each proven by its own law: `smf.deletion.keyed` (itself), `amf.takeover.authenticated` (`amf.indexes.unique`), `amf.release.forgets_ue` (`amf.sessions.owned`), `smf.establish.failure_releases` (`smf.sessions.known`) and `state.ids.free` (itself and `amf.sessions.owned`). Everything else a ticket states is at least as strong in `kernel/LAWS.bend`.
